# Dirt & Gravel Routes — 2–4 Days

**Summary**: Real bikepacking rather than paved touring — dirt, gravel, rural, remote.
Driving to a start point is fine, so this isn't limited to what's rideable out of
Portland. Trips of 2–4 days.

**Sources**: bikepacking.com route database, Exploring Wild, Oregon Timber Trail —
researched 2026-08-18.

**Last updated**: 2026-08-18

## 1. Frog Lake Loop ⭐ best first dirt trip

**45 mi · 2 days · 2,998 ft climbing · high point 4,161 ft · 82% unpaved, 13% singletrack**
**Difficulty 4/10** · climbing rated "fair" at 67 ft/mi · **99% rideable**

Starts and finishes at **Frog Lake, Mt. Hood National Forest** — the closest genuine
dirt route to Portland. Short enough that a slow pace still finishes early, and the
13% singletrack is a real taste of trail riding without committing to a full MTB route.

- **Camping**: Timothy Lake has six campgrounds plus **dispersed camping on NF-4280**.
  Frog Lake itself is reservable and reportedly less crowded.
- **Water**: Frog Lake, Clear Lake, Timothy Lake — late May to late September.
- **Bike**: *"Gravel bike with 34mm tires at minimum."* A mountain bike works if you
  want more of the singletrack.
- **Season**: late spring to early fall, snow-dependent.

**Why this one first**: lowest mileage, lowest difficulty, nearly all rideable, water
throughout, dispersed camping so no reservation pressure, and close enough that bailing
is easy. [Route →](https://bikepacking.com/routes/frog-lake-loop/)

## 2. Lower Deschutes Roller

**119 mi · 2 days · 9,737 ft climbing · 52% unpaved, 0% singletrack · Difficulty 6/10**

High desert on both sides of the Deschutes River, in Wasco and Sherman counties. Starts
at **Deschutes River State Recreation Area**, ~90 miles east of Portland. Snow-capped
volcanoes across open country — the most dramatic scenery on this list.

- **Resupply**: Grass Valley Country Market on day one. **Day two has nothing until the
  river.** Logistics rated *strenuous* even though the riding is technically easy.
- **Water**: the Deschutes, filtered.
- **Camping**: Twin Springs Campground and multiple BLM sites along the river.
- **Bike**: 42mm+ tyres; testers ran 38–48mm.

⚠️ **Real hazards here**: western rattlesnakes from early spring to late fall, poison
oak along the river, no shade, stiff headwinds, spotty cell service. **Ride it spring or
fall — not summer.** Bring a tail light for the highway sections.
[Route →](https://bikepacking.com/routes/lower-deschutes-roller-overnighter/)

## 3. Anaxshat Passage — Oregon Timber Trail, Hood Tier Loop

**120 mi · 3–4 days · ~10,000 ft climbing · 47% paved, 40% dirt road, 13% singletrack**

A loop of the Oregon Timber Trail's Hood Tier, following parts of the historic Skyline
Trail from near Mount Jefferson toward Mount Hood. Singletrack along the Clackamas
River, backroads, an alpine lakeshore trail, and one rough jeep road.

At four days that's ~30 miles a day. Described as **geared toward less-experienced
mountain bikers**, and importantly *"the trail sections aren't mandatory — there are
easy detours if you prefer to stick to roads."* That escape hatch makes it a sensible
step up rather than a leap.

[Route →](https://bikepacking.com/routes/anaxshat-passage/) ·
[Oregon Timber Trail →](https://oregontimbertrail.org/hood-tier-loop-anaxshat-passage)

## 4. Pine Mountain Observatory Loop

**79 mi · 2 days** · high desert forest east of Bend — an overnight sampler of the
Oregon Outback country. Won the Bikepacking Collective's **"Choice Overnighter" of
2021**. Longer drive (~3h30m to Bend), so better paired with other Bend riding than done
as a standalone weekend.

## 5. Cascade Skyline

**173 mi · 4 days** · classic gravel and dirt roads through what bikepacking.com calls
Oregon's most iconic scenery. The biggest of the shortlist and the one to graduate to.
[Route →](https://bikepacking.com/routes/the-cascade-skyline/)

## Ruled out for now

Not because they're bad — because they don't fit 2–4 days:

| Route | Miles | Days |
|---|---|---|
| Central Oregon Backcountry Explorer | 152 | 3–5 (beginner-rated, borderline — worth a look) |
| Elkhorn Crest Trail | 52 | 2 — but eastern Oregon, ~5h drive |
| Three Sisters Three Rivers | 325 | 9 |
| The Oregon Outback | 364 | 6 |
| Oregon Timber Trail (full) | 688 | 25 |
| Steens Mountain Loop | 276 | 3–4 — but remote SE Oregon, 90°F+, scarce water |

**Central Oregon Backcountry Explorer** is the near-miss worth revisiting: 152 miles,
beginner-rated, a Prineville loop, mostly gravel, with the donation-based Spoke'n Hostel
in Mitchell partway. Resupply in Ashwood and Mitchell. Tubeless strongly advised —
**thorn punctures are the known problem out there**.
