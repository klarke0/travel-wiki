# Beginner Overnight Bikepacking Routes Near Portland

**Summary**: Researched beginner-friendly overnight/short bikepacking routes within a short drive (or direct ride) of Portland, OR — for a first overnight trip. Originally researched for the Aug 7-9, 2026 3-day weekend.

**Sources**: WebSearch — bikepacking.com, Travel Oregon, Oregon State Parks, Urban Adventure League, Willamette Week, NPS — added 2026-07-27.

**Last updated**: 2026-07-27

---

**Weather note**: Early August is peak Oregon dry season — expect warm, sunny, low-precip conditions on all routes below. Main risks are heat/sun exposure and wildfire smoke (check AQI before departure), not rain.

## 1. Banks-Vernonia Trail → L.L. Stub Stewart State Park ⭐ Recommended for a first trip

- **Distance**: Trail is 21 miles one-way (Banks to Vernonia); Stub Stewart sits ~5-7 miles from the Banks end, so an easy overnight is ~10-15 miles round trip if camping partway, or the full 21 miles one-way with a shuttle/return.
- **Terrain**: Paved, converted rail-trail, minimal elevation change, shaded — the easiest, most forgiving surface of any option here. Well suited to a gravel-capable bike.
- **Camping**: L.L. Stub Stewart State Park — 12 walk-in tent sites, cabins, and (seasonally) 21 primitive hike-in sites at Brooke Creek Camp. Reservable up to 6 months out via [oregonstateparks.reserveamerica.com](https://oregonstateparks.reserveamerica.com) — **book soon, Aug 7-9 is a summer weekend and sites fill**. Has showers, restrooms, water, EV/bike charging.
- **Getting there**: ~34 miles west of Portland by car to the Banks trailhead — not realistically rideable directly from the city.
- **Water/resupply**: Water available in the park; small-town resupply in Banks or Vernonia.
- **Hazards**: Very few — no major road crossings, flat and shaded. The safest option on this list.
- **Source**: [L.L. Stub Stewart State Park](https://stateparks.oregon.gov/index.cfm?do=park.profile&parkId=75), [Banks-Vernonia State Trail — Travel Oregon](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/destinations/parks-forests-wildlife-areas/banks-vernonia-state-trail/)

## 2. Crown Zellerbach Trail (Scappoose to Vernonia)

- **Distance**: 21 miles one-way; overnight at Scaponia Park (roughly mid-route) shortens day one.
- **Terrain**: Mixed pavement/gravel — paved near Scappoose, transitioning to gravel/broken pavement further west. More varied than Banks-Vernonia, still non-technical.
- **Camping**: Scaponia Park or Vernonia Lake campground — both explicitly called out as beginner-friendly bikepacking camps, restrooms available.
- **Getting there**: ~30-minute drive northwest of Portland to the Scappoose trailhead.
- **Water/resupply**: Limited once past Scappoose — carry water; small resupply in Vernonia at the far end.
- **Hazards**: Trail surface degrades (looser gravel, less maintained) toward the western end — fine for a gravel bike, not a road bike. More isolated/less trafficked than Banks-Vernonia.
- **Source**: [Crown Zellerbach Trail — Travel Oregon](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/outdoor-recreation/bicycling/hike-or-ride-the-crown-zellerbach-trail/), [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/crown-zellerbach-trail.htm)

## 3. Historic Columbia River Highway → Ainsworth State Park (Columbia Gorge)

- **Distance**: ~35-39 miles one-way from a Portland MAX station/Troutdale via Marine Drive Trail + Historic Highway.
- **Terrain**: Paved historic highway with a real climb — ~900 ft up to Vista House, ~1,500 ft total gain. More physically demanding than the trail options but still paved, no technical riding.
- **Camping**: Ainsworth State Park Campground — dedicated hiker/biker campsite (**no reservations required, first-come**) plus 6 walk-in tent sites, showers/restrooms, flush toilets, cell service, camp host, firewood for sale. Well-established, actively used bike-overnight destination (local group Urban Adventure League runs recurring trips here, most recently July 2026).
- **Getting there**: Can be ridden directly from Portland (Marine Drive/Troutdale) — no car shuttle needed, a plus for a fully self-supported first trip.
- **Water/resupply**: Troutdale (early in the ride) has full resupply; limited services after that until the park.
- **Hazards**: Historic Highway has popular tourist-vehicle traffic near the waterfalls (Multnomah, Horsetail) — busier road sections than the rail-trail options. Some nearby hiking trails were fire-closed in older reports — worth confirming current status closer to the date.
- **Source**: [Urban Adventure League — Bike Overnight Ainsworth](https://urbanadventureleague.wordpress.com/2026/07/21/bike-overnight-camping-at-ainsworth-columbia-gorge-13-14-july-2026/), [Willamette Week — 5 Campsites](https://www.wweek.com/culture/2019/04/10/five-campsites-you-can-bike-to-from-portland-without-suffering/)

## 4. Milo McIver State Park (easiest/shortest, ride-from-city)

- **Distance**: 25 miles one-way from Johnson Creek MAX Station.
- **Terrain**: Entirely flat bike paths and low-traffic roads — the mellowest terrain of any option, described as "one of the chillest bikepacking day trips."
- **Camping**: Reservable tent sites with showers, restrooms, bike repair stations, solar charging.
- **Getting there**: Rideable directly from Portland via MAX + bike paths — no car needed.
- **Water/resupply**: Standard park facilities; towns along the Clackamas River route.
- **Hazards**: None significant — lowest-risk, lowest-effort option. Arguably tame if you want a "real" bikepacking feel, but ideal as a confidence-building first trip.
- **Source**: [Willamette Week — 5 Campsites You Can Bike to From Portland](https://www.wweek.com/culture/2019/04/10/five-campsites-you-can-bike-to-from-portland-without-suffering/)

## Dispersed / Stealth Camping Option (Kevin is open to this — expands the choices)

If avoiding reservations/fees entirely appeals, dispersed camping is a real, **legal** option nearby — not sketchy, just unofficial and amenity-free:

- **Tillamook State Forest** — right next to the Banks-Vernonia corridor (Stub Stewart sits inside/adjacent to it). Dispersed camping is **allowed year-round, no fee, no permit** under Oregon Dept. of Forestry rules. Stay limit 14 days per 30. No facilities (no water, no toilets, no trash service) — full Leave No Trace required. Campfires banned during Regulated Use fire season (likely in effect for early August — check current ODF fire restrictions before the trip). Camp 200 ft from water sources, and watch for private land intermixed with public forest — look for boundary signage. Access roads can be steep/narrow gravel in spots.
- **Mt. Hood National Forest** — free dispersed camping, easily accessible from Portland, a broader option if the Gorge routes are the pick.
- **Gifford Pinchot National Forest** (WA side, near the Gorge) — dispersed sites near the Columbia Gorge routes, but most require a Northwest Forest Pass or America the Beautiful Pass, and Forest Service patrols the area — less "stealth," more "legitimately free but permitted."
- **Practical note from research**: stealth/dispersed camping etiquette is to avoid camping in open clearings/meadows and tuck into tree cover so the site isn't visible from trails — same practice whether or not it's technically permitted.

This actually **removes the booking urgency** for options #1 and #3 — Ainsworth's hiker/biker site needs no reservation at all, and Banks-Vernonia has a legal no-permit dispersed alternative in Tillamook State Forest immediately adjacent if Stub Stewart's reservable sites are full or you'd rather skip the fee.

## Routes Checked But Not Recommended

- **Sandy Ridge** — this is a singletrack MTB flow-trail system, not a bikepacking route. No on-site camping (nearest is 15-30 min drive away), terrain skews technical MTB, not gravel-bike-appropriate.
- **Springwater Corridor / Sauvie Island** — no legitimate overnight camping option tied to either. Sauvie Island has no public overnight camping; Springwater is a day-use urban path. Fine as in-town connectors, not standalone destinations.

## Recommendation

**Banks-Vernonia Trail → Tillamook State Forest dispersed camping (near Stub Stewart) (#1)** for a true first overnight — most forgiving terrain of any option (smooth paved rail-trail, negligible elevation, no traffic hazards), and since Kevin's open to dispersed camping, this drops the reservation/fee entirely while staying legal. Stub Stewart's official reservable sites remain the fallback if a specific spot with showers/water is preferred.

If more "adventure" feel is wanted, with a bike-only departure from the city (no car shuttle): **Ainsworth State Park via the Historic Columbia River Highway (#3)** — well-documented, actively used by a local bikepacking group, no reservation needed for the hiker/biker site, just more elevation and some tourist traffic to manage.
