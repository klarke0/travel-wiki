# Bermuda

Bermuda in January is not the Bermuda on the postcard, and it's better to know that
going in than to find out on arrival.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Air** | 60–69°F. A light jacket morning and evening |
| **Sea** | **66°F — too cold to swim without a wetsuit** |
| **Season** | Off season. Rates are heavily discounted |
| **Flights from PDX** | **No direct. 10–17 hours each way** via east coast hubs |
| **Passport** | Required |

Also worth knowing: it's **not** in the Caribbean. Bermuda sits out in the North
Atlantic, level with South Carolina, which is exactly why it's cool in January.

---

## The honest case for and against

**Against.** The beaches are the reason to go and you can't use them. Water sports and
excursions run reduced schedules or shut down entirely for the winter. A few hotels close
for refurbishment. And from Portland it's a full travel day each way — on a three-night
weekend you'd spend more time in transit than on a beach you can't swim at anyway.

**For.** If you drop the beach premise, January Bermuda is a genuinely good cool-weather
trip: forts and caves, an 18-mile rail trail, a UNESCO town, pink sand beaches with
nobody on them, and restaurants you can actually get into. Off-season rates on properties
that are expensive the rest of the year. Stores, restaurants, bars and most sights stay
open as normal — it's the water-based stuff that stops.

**It comes down to what MLK-mas is.** If it's "the families sit somewhere warm together,"
Puerto Rico wins outright. If it's "go somewhere none of us has been and eat well,"
Bermuda is defensible and will cost less.

## Lodging for four

The four properties below all operate through January. **Check pool heating before
booking** — with a 66°F sea, a heated pool is the difference between swimming and not.

| Property | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rosewood Bermuda** | Tucker's Town | The island's **largest private pink sand beach**, heated pools. The most likely to make a January trip work |
| **Hamilton Princess & Beach Club** | Hamilton | Open since 1885, on the harbour, in town so you can walk to dinner. Some outlets are seasonal — 1609 Bar & Restaurant among them |
| **The Loren at Pink Beach** | South Shore | Small, modern, design-led. One of only 27 Sisley Paris spas in the world |
| **Cambridge Beaches** | Somerset, West End | Cottage colony on its own peninsula, 15 min from the Dockyard. Cottages suit four better than hotel rooms do |

Cambridge Beaches and the Rosewood cottages are the two that most naturally sleep four
without booking two separate rooms.

## Places to go

Weighted toward what actually works in cool weather.

### The Railway Trail
An 18-mile car-free path along the old Bermuda Railway bed, running most of the length of
the island. Walk it or rent bikes. **This is better in January than in August** — it's
exposed, and 65°F and breezy is ideal for it. The single best cool-weather thing on the
island.

### St George's
UNESCO World Heritage town at the east end, and one of the oldest continuously inhabited
English settlements in the Americas. Lanes, forts, **Tobacco Bay** just outside.

### Crystal Caves
Underground limestone caves with a floating pontoon walkway over a clear subterranean
lake. Entirely weather-proof, which is the point in January.

### Royal Naval Dockyard
West end. The old fortified naval base, now shops, the National Museum and the Clocktower.
Where the cruise ships dock — in January there are far fewer, which is a plus.

### The beaches, for looking at
**Horseshoe Bay** and **Warwick Long Bay** are the famous pink sand. Empty in January.
Perfectly good for a walk and photographs; just don't plan to be in the water.

### Eating
Hamilton is the centre of it. Off-season means you can get a table, which is not true in
July. Fish chowder with sherry pepper sauce and black rum is the thing to order.
