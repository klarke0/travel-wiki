# Old San Juan

The 500-year-old walled city. **The one thing on this trip everybody will enjoy**, and it
needs no car, no reservation and barely any planning.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Half day minimum, easily two |
| **Cost** | Small National Park Service fee for the forts |
| **Getting there** | Walkable from Condado, 20 min from the airport |

## The forts

**Castillo San Felipe del Morro (El Morro)** — the big one out on the point, six levels,
huge lawn where everyone flies kites. The postcard.

**Castillo San Cristóbal** — the larger of the two and usually much quieter. Tunnels,
sentry boxes, better views over the city. If El Morro is heaving, come here.

## The streets

**Calle Fortaleza** — the most famous street in the old city, pastel buildings, the
Governor's mansion at the end. This is the one with the hanging umbrellas in photos.

**Calle San Sebastián** — where you go at night. Bar after bar, busiest street after dark.

**Paseo de la Princesa** — the promenade along the outside of the city wall, running down
to the **San Juan Gate**, one of the original 1635 entrances and 16 feet tall.

**La Perla** — the brightly painted neighbourhood clinging to the cliff outside the wall.
Photograph it from above; it's a residential community, not an attraction.

## Buildings worth stepping into

- **Catedral de San Juan Bautista** — the oldest church on US soil
- **La Fortaleza** — the Governor's mansion, oldest executive mansion in continuous use
  in the Americas
- **Casa Blanca** — built for Ponce de León's family

## Eating and drinking

**Barrachina**, on Calle Fortaleza, claims to be where the piña colada was invented. The
claim is contested and the drink is fine. Go for the story.

The old city has a serious restaurant scene now. **Book anything good over a holiday
weekend**, it will not be walk-in.

## Also right there

**Casa Bacardí** is across the bay in Cataño, reachable by a cheap ferry from the old
city. Distillery tour, tasting, and the ferry ride itself is a nice bit of the day.
